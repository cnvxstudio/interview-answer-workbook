# Interview Question and Follow-up Cards

These are original practice prompts, not questions attributed to a particular employer. Use a real example you can discuss safely. For a situation you have not experienced, clearly say that your answer is hypothetical.

Choose a main question first. A partner can select one of its follow-ups after hearing the answer.

## 1. Explain your work

**Main question:** Tell me about a project you worked on and the part you owned.

- What would not have happened without your contribution?
- Which detail would someone outside your field need explained?

## 2. Prioritize competing requests

**Main question:** Describe a time you had more requests than you could complete. How did you choose what came first?

- What evidence supported that choice?
- What would have made you choose differently?

## 3. Handle disagreement

**Main question:** Tell me about a disagreement over how to solve a problem.

- What did the other person understand that you initially missed?
- How did you reach a decision if you still disagreed?

## 4. Respond to a mistake

**Main question:** Describe a mistake you made and how you responded.

- How did you recognize the mistake?
- What changed in your process afterward, and what remains uncertain?

## 5. Work with incomplete information

**Main question:** Tell me about a decision you had to make before you had all the information you wanted.

- Which assumption carried the most risk?
- What was the smallest useful check you could do first?

## 6. Product management

**Main question:** A team wants to build a feature because several people requested it. What would you investigate before recommending it?

- How would you distinguish a repeated request from a problem worth solving?
- What evidence would make you stop or change the proposal?

## 7. Software engineering

**Main question:** Walk me through how you would investigate an intermittent failure you cannot immediately reproduce.

- What would you inspect first, and why?
- How would you verify a fix without claiming more than the evidence shows?

## 8. Customer support

**Main question:** How would you handle a customer whose problem you cannot resolve during the first conversation?

- What would you communicate before ending the conversation?
- How would you avoid promising a resolution time you cannot control?

## 9. Design

**Main question:** Describe a design choice that involved a meaningful trade-off.

- What alternative did you consider?
- What feedback or observation would lead you to revise it?

## 10. Learn something unfamiliar

**Main question:** Tell me about a time you had to learn something unfamiliar to finish a task.

- How did you decide what was essential to learn first?
- What showed that you could use it, rather than only explain it?

## Partner instructions

1. Let the speaker finish the first answer.
2. Ask one follow-up, rather than several questions at once.
3. Identify one specific detail that helped you understand the answer.
4. Identify one gap as a question: “What did you personally decide?” is more useful than “Be more specific.”
5. Give the speaker a chance to revise. Discuss the answer, not their value as a candidate.
